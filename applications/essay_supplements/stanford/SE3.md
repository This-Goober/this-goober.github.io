## Please describe what aspects of your life experiences, interests, and character would help you make a distinctive contribution as an undergraduate to Stanford University.

Selina and I began with a good idea and an unusable system. We wanted stronger high-school musicians to mentor middle-school beginners. In our first pilot, one mentor sometimes faced about ten students. Good intentions did not solve recruiting or attendance, especially when one mentor was trying to watch ten sets of hands.

That summer, Selina and I rebuilt the program together. We made signup forms, a shared Drive, attendance rules, clearer goals, and a way for reliable mentors to receive volunteer credit. We asked our orchestra director to let us present in classes instead of waiting for people to find us. The program grew to roughly fifteen mentors and twenty beginners across three eight-week terms.

The numbers are not the part I want to contribute. I like staying after the exciting idea to build what lets other people use it. Our next problem is mentor preparation, because playing well does not automatically teach someone how to notice a beginner’s needs. I want the next student who opens that shared Drive to find instructions they can improve, not a monument to the people who made it.
