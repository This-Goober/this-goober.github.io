# Harvard University — Psychology A.B. (Cognitive Science MBB Track) + Harvard–NEC A.B./M.M.
**Program Dossier · Prepared for Eidee Tan & Family · Fall 2027 Entry · Research date: August 15, 2026**

> This pathway pairs Harvard College's **Psychology concentration, Cognitive Science track in Mind, Brain, and Behavior (MBB)** — a thesis-required, interdisciplinary cognitive-science program that reaches into computer science, linguistics, philosophy, and neuroscience — with the **Harvard–New England Conservatory dual degree**, a five-year program awarding a Harvard A.B. plus an NEC Master of Music. The single most important thing to know: the NEC overlay requires **separate, independent admission to both Harvard College and NEC in the same cycle**, including an NEC prescreening video due **December 1** and a **live audition in Boston in February**, and NEC enrolls only **five to six dual-degree students per year**. The Psychology/MBB side, by contrast, has no admission gate beyond getting into Harvard: students declare the concentration in sophomore fall, and the track's one hard internal commitment is a **required senior honors thesis**.

## 2. The big picture — what kind of place this is

Harvard College is a liberal-arts college embedded in the world's most heavily resourced research university. Undergraduates live in twelve residential Houses, choose from more than 50 concentrations and 3,700 courses, and declare their field in the **fall of sophomore year** — a full year of exploration is built into the design ([Advising Programs Office](https://advising.college.harvard.edu/academic-planning-for-students/concentrations-and-secondaries/)). The curriculum is neither a rigid core nor fully open: every student completes four **General Education** courses, one in each of Aesthetics & Culture; Ethics & Civics; Histories, Societies, Individuals; and Science & Technology in Society, alongside the College's divisional distribution and related requirements ([Gen Ed FAQs](https://gened.college.harvard.edu/faqs/)). Two of those Gen Ed categories — Ethics & Civics and Science & Technology in Society — map almost perfectly onto a cognition-to-technology-law direction.

For a student aiming at cognitive science as an intellectual home and technology law as a destination, Harvard's distinctive asset is the **Mind, Brain, and Behavior (MBB) Initiative**: a university-wide interdisciplinary community that threads through seven concentrations (Psychology, Computer Science, Linguistics, Philosophy, Neuroscience, History and Science, and Human Biology, Behavior & Evolution), with shared foundational courses, interdisciplinary seminars, a junior symposium, and a required senior thesis, culminating in a **Certificate in Mind Brain Behavior** awarded alongside the A.B. ([MBB tracks](https://mbb.harvard.edu/pages/tracks)). Around it sits an unusually deep law-and-technology ecosystem — the Berkman Klein Center for Internet & Society at Harvard Law School, and the Embedded EthiCS collaboration between the philosophy and computer science faculties — described in Section 6.

Music at Harvard is large, serious, and mostly extracurricular. The Office for the Arts supports roughly **45 music groups**, including the Harvard-Radcliffe Orchestra and the Bach Society Orchestra, and the Music Department offers credit-bearing performance courses in which professionals coach chamber ensembles ([Music Department, prospective students](https://music.fas.harvard.edu/academics/undergraduate-study-in-music-information-for-prospective-students)). For a musician who wants conservatory-level training on top of a Harvard degree, the College maintains what it calls "very competitive" dual programs with the **New England Conservatory** and Berklee ([same page](https://music.fas.harvard.edu/academics/undergraduate-study-in-music-information-for-prospective-students)) — the NEC route is covered in full in Section 3.

## 3. The program — Psychology A.B., Cognitive Science (MBB) track + Harvard–NEC A.B./M.M.

### 3a. Psychology A.B., Cognitive Science track in Mind, Brain, and Behavior

**Exact name and home unit:** Psychology concentration (A.B.), Cognitive Science (MBB) track, Department of Psychology, Faculty of Arts & Sciences. Psychology offers three tracks — **General**, **Cognitive Neuroscience & Evolutionary Psychology (CNEP, the "Life Sciences" track)**, and **Cognitive Science (MBB)** ([Psychology tracks](https://undergrad.psychology.fas.harvard.edu/tracks)).

**Who it's for:** students who want cognition studied across disciplines rather than inside one department. The department describes the MBB track as allowing students to "combine coursework in Psychology with courses from related MBB curriculum including Linguistics, Philosophy, Human Evolutionary Biology, History and Science, Neuroscience, and Computer Science," with less required biology than CNEP and a broader range of fields counting toward concentration requirements ([Cognitive Science (MBB) overview](https://undergrad.psychology.fas.harvard.edu/mbb)).

**Structure and course counts.** Per the department's requirements chart (August 2025 edition, latest published at research date), the base Psychology concentration is 12 courses; the thesis sequence brings the total to **14 courses**, and **the MBB track requires the thesis** — a student who decides not to write one must switch tracks ([requirements chart](https://undergrad.psychology.fas.harvard.edu/requirements-chart); [MBB overview](https://undergrad.psychology.fas.harvard.edu/mbb)). The pieces:

| Component | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Introductory | **PSY 1** (Introduction to Psychological Science) |
| Foundational | **NEURO 80** (neurobiology; formerly MCB 81) plus one of PSY 11 / 14 / 15 / 16 / 18 |
| Statistics & methods | **PSY 1900** (statistics) and **PSY 1901** (research methods, by end of fifth semester) |
| Sophomore tutorial | **PSY 971** |
| Advanced courses | **Four**: three "MBB Advanced Courses" drawn from Computer Science, History and Science, Human Evolutionary Biology, Linguistics, Neuroscience, and Philosophy; one Psychology departmental advanced course ([MBB track courses](https://undergrad.psychology.fas.harvard.edu/cognitive-science-mbb-track-courses)) |
| MBB Junior Seminar | One 980-level interdisciplinary MBB seminar ([MBB track courses](https://undergrad.psychology.fas.harvard.edu/cognitive-science-mbb-track-courses)) |
| Thesis sequence | One lab course for credit + yearlong senior thesis tutorial **PSY 991A/991B**; thesis application filed and approved in the sixth semester |
| Non-credit MBB events | **Junior-year Interdisciplinary Symposium** and **senior-year Interdisciplinary Research Workshop**, required of all MBB students university-wide ([MBB tracks](https://mbb.harvard.edu/pages/tracks)) |

**What makes it coherent:** the three MBB advanced courses are where Eidee would build the computation-and-mind spine — CS courses on AI and computation count toward the *psychology* concentration, which is rare. The thesis then forces synthesis: MBB theses must be "on a topic in mind brain behavior that has been approved by their concentration" ([MBB tracks](https://mbb.harvard.edu/pages/tracks)), and a project on, say, human trust in machine advice or cognitive assumptions embedded in AI regulation is legible both as cognitive science and as pre-law technology-policy work.

**How MBB differs from regular Psychology:** (1) the thesis is mandatory, not an honors option; (2) three advanced courses come from *outside* the Psychology department by design; (3) students join a university-wide cohort across seven concentrations, with its own seminars, symposium, and certificate. General-track students have none of these obligations — and none of the structure.

### 3b. The Harvard–NEC dual degree (A.B. + M.M., five years)

**Exact name and units:** Harvard/New England Conservatory Dual Degree Program — Harvard College A.B. (or S.B.) in any concentration + **Master of Music from NEC** ([Harvard Music Department program page](https://music.fas.harvard.edu/harvard-university-and-new-england-conservatory-music-dual-degree-program); [NEC undergraduate programs](https://necmusic.edu/admissions/degree-programs/undergraduate-programs/)). The undergraduate concentration can be Psychology/MBB — the dual degree is an overlay, not a replacement major.

**Five-year structure** ([NEC undergraduate programs](https://necmusic.edu/admissions/degree-programs/undergraduate-programs/)):

- **Years 1–3:** full-time Harvard student, living at Harvard, combining Harvard academic coursework with **studio instruction at NEC (one hour per week)**. The campuses are ~2.5 miles apart — about 30 minutes by bus, 20 by car or bike ([Harvard Music Department](https://music.fas.harvard.edu/harvard-university-and-new-england-conservatory-music-dual-degree-program)).
- **Year 4:** complete the Harvard A.B. while beginning Master of Music requirements at NEC.
- **Year 5:** full-time at NEC, finishing the M.M.

Dual-degree students may join NEC performing ensembles "as their schedule permits" and remain free to take Harvard Music Department courses, pursue a music secondary, or even a joint concentration ([Harvard Music Department](https://music.fas.harvard.edu/harvard-university-and-new-england-conservatory-music-dual-degree-program)).

**Admission — two independent gates.** Applicants apply **separately** to Harvard College and to NEC, must be admitted **independently by each**, and check the "Harvard-NEC Dual-Degree" box on both applications; NEC makes the final call on dual-degree acceptance ([Harvard Music Department](https://music.fas.harvard.edu/harvard-university-and-new-england-conservatory-music-dual-degree-program)). Harvard requires no audition; **NEC requires a live on-campus audition in February from all dual-degree candidates**, judged to a *higher* standard than regular B.M. applicants ([same page](https://music.fas.harvard.edu/harvard-university-and-new-england-conservatory-music-dual-degree-program)). NEC's published cycle (current published dates; confirm when the 2026–27 cycle posts): application and **prescreening video due December 1**; recommendations January 15; prescreen-passing candidates invited to February live auditions; decisions late March ([NEC undergraduate applicants](https://necmusic.edu/admissions/undergraduate-applicants/); [NEC audition requirements](https://necmusic.edu/admissions/audition-requirements/) — violin repertoire is listed by department on that page's selector). A safety valve exists: a first-year Harvard student who has never applied to NEC may apply to enter the program during the first year; students rejected by either school or by the program may not reapply ([Harvard Music Department](https://music.fas.harvard.edu/harvard-university-and-new-england-conservatory-music-dual-degree-program)).

**Scale:** NEC describes the program as "one of the most competitive dual-degree programs in the world," with **"only five to six students enrolling each year"** ([NEC undergraduate programs](https://necmusic.edu/admissions/degree-programs/undergraduate-programs/)).

**Costs.** During years 1–4 students pay **Harvard tuition and fees** (potentially reduced per-course for NEC credits transferred), plus an **NEC tuition covering the cost of studio instruction** (with an additional fee for NEC credits beyond studio instruction); in **year 5 students pay NEC's Master of Music tuition** ([Harvard Music Department](https://music.fas.harvard.edu/harvard-university-and-new-england-conservatory-music-dual-degree-program)). NEC's 2026–27 graduate tuition is **$64,208** plus ~$2,000 in fees before housing; NEC reports 90% of its students receive scholarship support, based on need and artistic merit, via its institutional aid application and FAFSA ([NEC tuition + aid](https://necmusic.edu/admissions/tuition-aid/)). Each institution's aid office handles only its own charges — Harvard's generous aid does not extend to NEC bills.

## 4. How the pieces fit together

Harvard admission is to the College as a whole — no major is declared at application, and the concentration choice is made in **sophomore fall** with no competitive screen for Psychology ([Advising Programs Office](https://advising.college.harvard.edu/academic-planning-for-students/concentrations-and-secondaries/)). The MBB track is entered through the Psychology concentration; its one mid-course commitment is the **sixth-semester thesis application**. One secondary field may be added: **Computer Science** (four Harvard CS courses, at least two advanced, with breadth tags; at most one course may double-count with the concentration — [CS secondary requirements](https://csadvising.seas.harvard.edu/secondary)) is the natural complement, since it certifies the technical depth that Psychology/MBB alone does not require. Psychology also offers its own secondary for students concentrating elsewhere ([Psychology secondary field](https://undergrad.psychology.fas.harvard.edu/secondary-field)). Note that **MBB itself is not a secondary field** — it is a track-plus-certificate inside participating concentrations.

Scheduling reality: 14 concentration courses + 4 Gen Eds + distribution requirements fit comfortably in 32 courses over eight semesters, with room for CS50, statistics, and Gov or philosophy electives. Adding the NEC overlay costs weekly travel to Boston for studio, NEC ensemble time, and personal practice — the real constraint is hours, not course slots.

> **Gates beyond Harvard admission**
> - **Psychology concentration / MBB track declaration** — *requirements-based, not competitive* (sophomore fall declaration; C- minimum in PSY 1900; thesis application approved in sixth semester).
> - **Senior thesis** — *requirements-based*; mandatory for MBB (leave the track if not pursued).
> - **NEC dual degree** — *competitive, twice over*: independent NEC admission (Dec 1 application + prescreen video, February live audition at a higher-than-B.M. standard) and final dual-degree selection by NEC; ~5–6 enrollees/year.
> - **OFA Music Lesson Subsidy** — *requirements-based with proficiency review*; only for financial-aid recipients; **not available to dual-degree students** ([OFA subsidy page](https://ofa.fas.harvard.edu/music-lesson-subsidy-program)).
> - **Berkman Klein internships, lab RA positions** — *competitive applications; never guaranteed*.

## 6. Beyond the classroom

**The MBB community** is the built-in research network: interdisciplinary seminars, the junior symposium, and the senior research workshop put Eidee in a cross-departmental cohort of thesis-writing students from Psychology, CS, Linguistics, Philosophy, and Neuroscience ([MBB tracks](https://mbb.harvard.edu/pages/tracks)). Thesis lab placement is arranged with individual faculty — abundant but applicant-driven.

**Law, policy, and AI ethics.** The **Berkman Klein Center for Internet & Society** at Harvard Law School runs a paid summer internship program open to undergraduates (2026 cycle: $21/hour, application due March 22, with tracks in technical and non-technical AI research) — genuinely accessible to Harvard College students but competitive and re-announced each year ([Berkman Klein Summer 2026 internships](https://cyber.harvard.edu/getinvolved/summer-2026-internship-program)). **Embedded EthiCS**, a joint venture of the philosophy and CS faculties, weaves philosopher-taught ethics modules (privacy, discrimination in ML, accessibility) directly into standard CS courses, so ethics arrives with the technical material rather than as an elective ([Embedded EthiCS](https://embeddedethics.seas.harvard.edu/about)). In Government, the **Tech Science program of study** (Gov 1430 "Tech Science to Save the World," Gov 1433 "From Democracy to Technocracy and Back Again," plus a practicum) is designed for Government concentrators — Eidee could not complete the program inside a Psychology concentration, but the individual Gov courses are regular Harvard courses she can take as electives, subject to normal enrollment ([Tech Science requirements](https://www.gov.harvard.edu/undergraduate/programs-of-study/tech-science/tech-science-requirements-and-course-options/)).

**Violin life.** Outside NEC, Harvard offers ~45 OFA-supported ensembles — including the **Harvard-Radcliffe Orchestra** and Bach Society Orchestra — plus credit-bearing chamber-music courses coached by master performers in the Music Department ([prospective-student music page](https://music.fas.harvard.edu/academics/undergraduate-study-in-music-information-for-prospective-students)). The department does **not** provide regular private lessons; instead, the **OFA Music Lesson Subsidy Program** supports lessons with outside teachers for full-time undergraduates *receiving financial aid*: typically **50–60% of lesson costs, averaging ~$400/semester for up to six lessons**, renewed on demonstrated follow-through — explicitly not open to Harvard/NEC dual-degree students, and not for beginners ([OFA Music Lesson Subsidy Program](https://ofa.fas.harvard.edu/music-lesson-subsidy-program)). Music concentrators receive separate departmental lesson funding from sophomore year.

## 7. Access, feasibility, and honest risks

**The NEC gate is very narrow.** Five to six enrollees a year, a February live-audition requirement judged above B.M. standard, and no reapplication after a rejection ([NEC undergraduate programs](https://necmusic.edu/admissions/degree-programs/undergraduate-programs/); [Harvard Music Department](https://music.fas.harvard.edu/harvard-university-and-new-england-conservatory-music-dual-degree-program)). The plan must be excellent *without* it: if NEC does not happen, the first-year application window is a second chance, and Harvard's own music life plus the lesson subsidy is the fallback — though the subsidy (~$400/semester) funds far less instruction than a conservatory studio.

**The MBB thesis is a commitment, not an option.** A student who reaches junior year and does not want a thesis must leave the track ([MBB overview](https://undergrad.psychology.fas.harvard.edu/mbb)). Relatedly, Psychology/MBB requires no programming or advanced math — the technical depth a future AI/IP lawyer needs must be self-imposed via the CS secondary or deliberate electives.

**No undergraduate law or policy concentration exists**, and the Government Tech Science *program* is closed to non-Gov concentrators; the law-and-technology layer is assembled from electives, Gen Ed, Berkman Klein-style opportunities, and the thesis topic. Research placements and internships are applications, not entitlements.

**When a different Harvard pathway would be better:** if institutions and governance come to outrank experimental cognition, the Government concentration with the Tech Science program of study becomes the stronger spine; if building systems outranks studying minds, Computer Science (which has its own MBB track — [MBB tracks](https://mbb.harvard.edu/pages/tracks)) inverts the recipe while keeping the MBB community and thesis.
