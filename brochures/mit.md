# Massachusetts Institute of Technology — Brain and Cognitive Sciences (Course 9) + STS Minor
**Program Dossier · Prepared for Eidee Tan & Family · Fall 2027 Entry · Research date: August 15, 2026**

> This pathway pairs MIT's Bachelor of Science in Brain and Cognitive Sciences ("Course 9") — a rigorous science-of-mind degree that already builds in Python, statistics, neural computation, and required laboratory research — with the six-subject minor in Science, Technology, and Society (STS), which supplies the historical, ethical, and political lens a future technology lawyer needs. It exists at MIT because the Department of Brain and Cognitive Sciences treats the mind as something to be explained mechanistically, and because MIT's humanities requirement is large enough (eight subjects) to hold a real second pillar. The single most important thing for the family to know: **admission is to MIT as a whole — students select any major freely at the end of the first year, with no internal admission gate** ([MIT Admissions FAQ](https://mitadmissions.org/help/faq/majors/)), so this entire academic plan is structurally available to every admitted student. The competitive gates come earlier (MIT admission itself, **4.6% admit rate for the Class of 2029**) and later (research placements and the music-lesson fellowship, both auditioned or applied-for).

## 2. The big picture — what kind of place this is

MIT is a science-and-engineering university with a maker's temperament: the motto "mens et manus" is lived out in a culture where understanding something means being able to build it, model it, or measure it. That culture extends to the study of the mind. MIT's Department of Brain and Cognitive Sciences describes its mission as understanding "how the mechanisms of the brain give rise to the mind," and its home, Building 46, as "the largest neuroscience research facility in the world," housing roughly 700 researchers together with the McGovern Institute for Brain Research and the Picower Institute for Learning and Memory ([About BCS](https://bcs.mit.edu/about-bcs)). For a student whose intellectual home is cognitive science and whose destination is technology law, this is a place to learn how intelligence — human and artificial — actually works, before arguing about how it should be governed.

Every undergraduate, regardless of major, completes the General Institute Requirements (GIRs): a six-subject science core (two calculus, two physics, one chemistry, one biology), **eight subjects in humanities, arts, and social sciences (HASS)** including a three-to-four-subject concentration, two Restricted Electives in Science and Technology (REST), a laboratory subject, four communication-intensive subjects (two CI-H, two in the major), and physical education with a swim test ([General Institute Requirements](https://catalog.mit.edu/mit/undergraduate-education/general-institute-requirements/)). The eight-subject HASS requirement is the structural reason an STS minor fits inside this degree rather than on top of it.

The first year is deliberately cushioned. Fall term is graded **Pass/No Record** — a C or better simply appears as "P," and anything below vanishes from the external transcript — with a credit limit of 54 units (plus 6 units of discovery subjects); spring moves to A/B/C/No Record with a 60-unit limit ([Credit Limits & Grading](https://firstyear.mit.edu/academics-exploration/credit-limits-grading/)). Students apply to MIT, not to a major or school: "Students apply to MIT for general admission and select a major at the end of the first year," and the "course of interest" named on the application carries no weight in admission ([Majors FAQ](https://mitadmissions.org/help/faq/majors/)). **There is no internal admission to Course 9 — declaring it is a free choice.**

What makes MIT uniquely strong for the cognition → AI → law direction is the combination of mechanistic depth and an institutionalized research pipeline. The Undergraduate Research Opportunities Program (UROP), founded in 1969, is the oldest program of its kind; **93% of the Class of 2025 did at least one UROP**, over 3,200 undergraduates participated in 2024–25, and MIT allocated $16.8 million to paid positions ([About UROP](https://urop.mit.edu/about/)). MIT's pre-law advising is explicit that "lawyers with a STEM background are at an advantage" in fields like intellectual property and AI law ([CAPD — Law](https://capd.mit.edu/channels/law/)).

Music at MIT is serious and heavily subscribed, not an afterthought. The Music and Theater Arts section runs auditioned ensembles — MIT Symphony Orchestra, Chamber Music Society, choruses, jazz — plus a merit-based private-lesson program (Emerson/Harris) and a full six-subject Music minor ([Auditions](https://mta.mit.edu/music/performance/auditions); [Music & Theater Arts catalog](https://catalog.mit.edu/schools/humanities-arts-social-sciences/music-theater-arts/)). Details in Section 6.

## 3. The program — Brain and Cognitive Sciences S.B. (Course 9) + STS minor

### Bachelor of Science in Brain and Cognitive Sciences (Course 9)
**Home unit:** Department of Brain and Cognitive Sciences, School of Science. All details below are from the [Course 9 degree chart](https://catalog.mit.edu/degree-charts/brain-cognitive-sciences-course-9/) in the 2025–26 catalog, the latest edition published at research time; the 2026–27 edition should be re-checked when it posts.

Course 9 is for students who want the science of mind and brain whole — psychology, neuroscience, and computation in one degree — rather than sliced into separate majors. The departmental program (168–174 units beyond the GIRs' contribution, out of 180 total units required beyond the GIRs) has four blocks:

**Required subjects (60 units).** Computation is mandatory, not optional: **6.100A Introduction to Computer Science Programming in Python** and **6.100B Introduction to Computational Thinking and Data Science** (6 units each); **9.00 Introduction to Psychological Science**; **9.01 Introduction to Neuroscience**; **9.40 Introduction to Neural Computation**; and a dedicated statistics subject, **9.07 Statistics for Brain and Cognitive Science** (or 18.05/18.600 from Mathematics).

**Tiered electives (seven subjects, 84 units).** Students "select seven subjects from the Tier II, Tier III, or Restricted Elective categories," with **at least three from Tier II** — the department's intermediate core. Signature choices relevant to Eidee's direction include **9.13 The Human Brain**, **9.35 Perception**, **9.19 Computational Psycholinguistics**, and **9.66 Computational Cognitive Science** ([Course 9 subject listing](https://catalog.mit.edu/subjects/9/)). This tier structure is what makes the major coherent rather than a pile of electives: breadth is forced at the intermediate level, then depth is chosen.

**Laboratory requirement (12 units).** One of **9.12** (Experimental Molecular Neurobiology), **9.17** (Systems Neuroscience Laboratory), **9.59[J]** (Laboratory in Psycholinguistics), or **9.60** (Machine-Motivated Human Vision) — all communication-intensive (CI-M), so scientific writing is built in.

**Research requirement (12–18 units).** A second, separate research experience chosen from the lab subjects above or from **9.41**, **9.50 Research in Brain and Cognitive Sciences**, or **9.URG Undergraduate Research** — meaning supervised original research (typically a UROP taken for credit) is a graduation requirement, not an extra. The same subject cannot satisfy both laboratory and research.

**Alternative worth knowing: Course 6-9, Computation and Cognition.** MIT also offers a joint **Bachelor of Science in Computation and Cognition (Course 6-9)** run by BCS with Electrical Engineering and Computer Science, requiring subjects such as 18.06 Linear Algebra and 6.3900 Introduction to Machine Learning alongside neuroscience ([Course 6-9 degree chart](https://catalog.mit.edu/degree-charts/computation-cognition-6-9/)). Choose 6-9 if building AI systems becomes the center of gravity; choose Course 9 if the human mind stays the object of study and computation remains the toolkit. For a cognitive-science-driven lawyer, Course 9 preserves more room for psychology, language, and perception — and more schedule room for STS. Switching between the two later is administratively straightforward since majors are freely declared.

### Minor in Science, Technology, and Society (STS)
**Home unit:** STS Program, School of Humanities, Arts, and Social Sciences. The minor is **six subjects (57–72 units)**, per the [STS section of the catalog](https://catalog.mit.edu/schools/humanities-arts-social-sciences/science-technology-society/) (2025–26): the gateway **STS.004 Intersections: Science, Technology, and the World**; one further Tier I subject — options include **STS.001 Technology in American History**, **STS.005[J] Data and Society**, **STS.006[J] Bioethics**, and **STS.012[J] Science in Action**; one subject from the more specialized Tier II list; and three additional electives drawn from either tier. There is no thesis; the coherence comes from the tier design — a common foundation, then themed depth (for Eidee, plausibly data, computing, and governance).

MIT-wide minor rules: "Minors consist of five to seven subjects, with a typical program comprising six"; students apply **by the end of sophomore year** and no later than Add Date one full term before graduation; at most **two minors**; and — crucial for scheduling — for a HASS minor like STS, **up to five of the six subjects may also count toward the eight-subject HASS requirement** ([Minors, MIT catalog](https://catalog.mit.edu/mit/undergraduate-education/academic-programs/minors/)). In practice the STS minor is largely *inside* the HASS requirement Eidee must complete anyway, which is why this combination is famously feasible.

## 4. How the pieces fit together

The load-bearing facts: (1) MIT admission is to the Institute, majors are **declared freely at the end of the first year** ([Majors FAQ](https://mitadmissions.org/help/faq/majors/)); (2) the STS minor rides mostly on the eight-subject HASS requirement, with up to five of six subjects double-counting ([Minors](https://catalog.mit.edu/mit/undergraduate-education/academic-programs/minors/)); (3) Course 9's own research requirement means a UROP-for-credit can satisfy a graduation requirement rather than compete with one ([Course 9 chart](https://catalog.mit.edu/degree-charts/brain-cognitive-sciences-course-9/)); (4) minor declaration is a form-based process aimed at the end of sophomore year — early enough to plan STS as a genuine second pillar. Scheduling reality: a typical term is four to five subjects (~48–60 units); Course 9 plus STS plus orchestra is a full but normal MIT load, protected in year one by Pass/No Record and credit limits ([Credit Limits & Grading](https://firstyear.mit.edu/academics-exploration/credit-limits-grading/)).

## 6. Beyond the classroom

**Research.** Course 9 students sit in the middle of the densest neuroscience-and-AI research corridor anywhere. Building 46 combines the BCS department with the **McGovern Institute for Brain Research** and the **Picower Institute for Learning and Memory** — about 700 researchers under one roof ([About BCS](https://bcs.mit.edu/about-bcs)). **CSAIL**, MIT's computer science and AI laboratory, and the **Media Lab** also host undergraduates; the Media Lab states plainly that "there are opportunities for MIT undergraduates to engage with the Media Lab" while granting no undergraduate degrees ([Media Lab — MIT undergraduates](https://www.media.mit.edu/graduate-program/mit-undergraduates/); [CSAIL research](https://www.csail.mit.edu/research)). Honest access note: all of these run on the UROP model — students find openings or pitch projects to faculty, "for pay or credit," with UROP staff support but no placement guarantee ([UROP guidelines](https://urop.mit.edu/guidelines/); [participation rules](https://urop.mit.edu/guidelines/participation-considerations/)). The 93% participation rate says access is normal, not exceptional — but a specific famous lab is never promised.

**Law, policy, ethics.** **MIT has no law school**, and no pre-law major — deliberately. Career Advising & Professional Development (CAPD) provides a dedicated pre-law advisor, a monthly pre-law newsletter, LSAT/LSAC guidance, and the student-run MIT Pre-Law Society; its advice is to major in what interests you most, noting the advantage STEM graduates hold in IP and AI law ([CAPD — Law](https://capd.mit.edu/channels/law/)). The STS Program itself is the in-house scholarly community for technology-and-society questions, with subjects like Data and Society and Bioethics ([STS catalog](https://catalog.mit.edu/schools/humanities-arts-social-sciences/science-technology-society/)).

**Violin and music life.** Three layers matter for Eidee:

- **Ensembles.** The **MIT Symphony Orchestra** admits by recorded audition (for the 2026–27 season: video with a ≤3-minute solo plus scales, due August 31); the **Chamber Music Society** auditions live in early September with "two solo pieces of contrasting styles and a prepared excerpt" ([Auditions](https://mta.mit.edu/music/performance/auditions)). Ensembles carry academic credit as performance subjects.
- **Private study — Emerson/Harris Program.** MIT's merit program funds private lessons for roughly 70 students a year at two levels. The **Scholars** level is "open to all full-time MIT students": after a Scholar pays for and completes 10 fall-semester lessons, **MIT matches the cost of 10 spring lessons (up to $1,200)**. The **Fellows** level is open to undergraduates "who have already completed one year of private study as a Scholar" (and to graduate students), with recital obligations; the page does not publish the Fellows award amount. **Auditions are held once a year, prior to the start of the fall semester**; awardees must register in an ensemble, attend a masterclass series, and perform in end-of-semester recitals ([Emerson/Harris Program](https://mta.mit.edu/music/performance/emersonharris-program-private-study)).
- **Academics.** A six-subject, 72-unit **Music minor** spans history/culture, composition/theory, and performance ([Music & Theater Arts catalog](https://catalog.mit.edu/schools/humanities-arts-social-sciences/music-theater-arts/)) — an option, not part of this pathway, but compatible with it as a second minor if desired (two minors maximum).
- **In admissions.** MIT accepts optional **creative portfolios in SlideRoom for performing artists** (and researchers/makers) as part of the first-year application ([Deadlines & Requirements](https://mitadmissions.org/apply/firstyear/deadlines-requirements/)) — the natural home for a violin recording and research abstracts.

**Funding.** UROP pays by the hour from a $16.8M annual pool or faculty grants (one funding source per term) ([UROP rules](https://urop.mit.edu/guidelines/participation-considerations/); [About UROP](https://urop.mit.edu/about/)).

## 7. Access, feasibility, and honest risks

**The admission gate is the pathway's dominant risk.** At 4.6% (Class of 2029), no profile makes MIT likely; everything in this dossier is conditional on that gate. After it, the structure is unusually open — no major admission, no minor competition — which is genuinely rare among Eidee's options.

**Workload physics.** MIT's science core (two physics, chemistry, biology) is non-negotiable and consumes much of year one. Course 9's math/statistics expectations are real, and 9.40 (Neural Computation) is a known step up in technical intensity. Pass/No Record absorbs the transition, but a student who wants heavy orchestra involvement plus a UROP must budget terms carefully.

**What is NOT included.** There is no law, pre-law, or public policy major for undergraduates in this pathway, and MIT has no law school; the legal-institutional side is carried by STS subjects, HASS concentration choices, and CAPD advising — strong scaffolding, but Eidee supplies the direction. The seed dossier's warning stands: STS must be treated as a genuine second pillar from year one, not a senior-year add-on, and Course 9 is a science degree, not an AI-engineering credential.

**Named-lab access.** UROP participation is near-universal, but placement in a specific celebrated lab (McGovern, CSAIL, Media Lab) depends on openings, fit, and persistence. Plan for "a strong cognition/AI UROP," not "a specific PI."

**Music funding is partial.** The Emerson/Harris Scholars award is a match up to $1,200 after a self-paid fall of lessons — meaningful but not full coverage, and auditioned annually.

**When a different MIT pathway would be better.** If building models comes to outrank studying minds, **Course 6-9 (Computation and Cognition)** or Course 6-14 (CS, Economics, and Data Science) plus the same STS minor would serve the AI-law direction with a heavier engineering signature; the free-declaration system means this decision can be made with a year of real information.
