Carnegie Mellon University — The BXA Intercollege Degrees: Music Performance (Violin) + an Academic Concentration

Program Dossier · Prepared for Eidee Tan & Family · Fall 2027 Entry · Research date: August 15, 2026

Carnegie Mellon is one of very few universities where an advanced violinist can make conservatory-level studio training half of a single bachelor’s degree while the other half is a serious computing, science, or cognition concentration. That is the BXA Intercollege Degree family: BCSA (Computer Science + Arts), BSA (Science + Arts), and BHA (Humanities + Arts) — all of which can carry a Music Performance concentration in violin, with private studio, orchestra, and music theory built into the degree, plus a two-semester capstone that must join both fields. The single most important thing to know: BXA applicants apply through the School of Music, face a violin prescreen (due December 1) and a live audition, and are reviewed only for that College of Fine Arts choice — indicating BXA interest does not guarantee a BXA offer, and Early Decision is not available for the School of Music. This dossier presents all three BXA variants as a decision family, and notes the non-audition fallback (Cognitive Science B.S. + SHIFT minor with violin as a top extracurricular).

2. The big picture — what kind of place this is

Carnegie Mellon began as Andrew Carnegie’s technical schools and grew into a research university with a peculiar, productive double identity: it is simultaneously one of the world’s most respected computing schools and home to a conservatory-style College of Fine Arts that has trained professional musicians, actors, and designers since 1905. Nowhere else in this research set do those two identities share a single campus quadrangle so literally — the School of Music sits a few hundred yards from the School of Computer Science — and the university has institutionalized the connection in the BXA Intercollege Degree Programs, sponsored jointly by five colleges: Fine Arts, Computer Science, Engineering, Dietrich (humanities and social sciences), and Mellon (science).

Undergraduates at CMU are admitted to a specific college, and each college sets its own general-education program. CMU counts coursework in “units” rather than credits (roughly 9–12 units per course; a normal semester is about 45–50 units), and its academic culture is famously work-centered — the unofficial motto really is “my heart is in the work.” That culture rewards students who arrive knowing what they want to build, and the BXA degrees are explicitly for students who refuse to choose between an art and an academic discipline.

For a cognition-to-AI-to-law trajectory, CMU’s strengths are foundational: it is a birthplace of artificial intelligence and cognitive science (Herbert Simon and Allen Newell did their Nobel- and Turing-recognized work here), the Center for the Neural Basis of Cognition is a joint CMU–University of Pittsburgh venture dating to 1994, and the Block Center for Technology and Society studies the economic, organizational, and policy impacts of AI. What CMU does not have is a law school; the law-and-policy layer is built from Dietrich’s philosophy and policy offerings — most directly the SHIFT minor described below.

Music at CMU is a professional school, not a campus amenity. Studio faculty include Pittsburgh Symphony Orchestra musicians; instrumental students play in auditioned major ensembles (Philharmonic, Wind Ensemble, Contemporary Ensemble, Opera Orchestra) as a curricular requirement. The BXA route places a violinist inside that professional environment at half-weight — which is exactly its appeal and exactly its cost in schedule and audition risk.

3. The program — BXA with a Music Performance (violin) concentration: BCSA, BSA, and BHA

All BXA degrees share one architecture: a BXA-specific general-education core, one College of Fine Arts concentration, one academic concentration in the partner college, a four-course BXA seminar sequence, and a two-semester senior capstone that must span both concentrations. The differences are which partner college supplies the academic half. (All unit figures below are from the 2025–26 catalog year pages — the latest published as of August 2026; Fall 2025 concentration sheets.)

The shared music half. The Music Performance concentration (Fall 2025 sheet) requires a minimum of 108 units: an 85-unit core — Harmony I (9), Solfège I (3), Eurhythmics I (3), Survey of Western Music History (9), Repertoire and Listening for Musicians (1), four semesters of BXA Studio (36 units) of private lessons, and four semesters of Major Ensemble (24 units) — plus at least 23 units of advisor-approved music electives. Admission to the performance track requires a live audition (BXA audition & review requirements), and for violin a prescreen video is mandatory first (details in §8). Music also offers BXA tracks in Composition, Audio Recording & Production, and Sound Theory & Practice, but Performance is the one that matches Eidee’s violin.

The shared connective tissue. The BXA seminar sequence is what makes this a designed degree rather than a stapled double major: 52-190 BXA Seminar I: Building the Wunderkammer and 52-291 Transferring Knowledge (4.5 units each, first-year/sophomore minis) teach interpretive theory and cross-disciplinary method; 52-392 Deconstructing Disciplines (9 units, junior spring) prepares the senior project; then the capstone pair — 52-401 Capstone Project Research (fall, 9 units) and 52-402 Capstone Project Production (spring, 9 units) — produces an original project spanning both concentrations, presented at the university-wide Meeting of the Minds research symposium each May. Capstones may be scholarly, creative, experimental, or computational — a music-cognition experiment, an interpretable-AI-for-performance system, a thesis on machine creativity.

The three variants on the table for Eidee

BCSA — Bachelor of Computer Science and Arts (CS + Music). Total 380 units: 122 gen-ed, a 117-unit School of Computer Science concentration, the 108–144-unit CFA concentration, 0–33 free electives (BCSA curriculum). The CS concentration (Fall 2025) is the real SCS core: 15-112 Fundamentals of Programming, then 15-122 Imperative Computation, 15-150 Functional Programming, 15-210 Parallel & Sequential Data Structures and Algorithms, 15-213 Computer Systems, 15-251 Great Ideas in Theoretical Computer Science, 21-127 Concepts of Mathematics, plus five further SCS courses at 200-level or above (AI/ML, robotics, HCI, graphics options). CMU describes BCSA students as wanting to “create the tools that make the art they want to make.” This is the strongest technical credential of the three — and the one where cognition, policy, and economics survive only in electives.

BSA — Bachelor of Science and Arts (a Mellon College of Science concentration + Music). Total 380 units: 129 gen-ed, a 114–145-unit MCS concentration, the CFA concentration, and 0–29 free electives (BSA curriculum). The allowed science concentrations are Biological Sciences, Chemistry, Environmental & Sustainability Studies, Mathematical Sciences, Neurobiology, and Physics. Note carefully: neither Computer Science nor Cognitive Science is a BSA option — CS lives in BCSA, and Cognitive Science lives in BHA. For Eidee, the only cognition-adjacent BSA concentration is Neurobiology, which is a wet-lab/brain-science framing rather than a computational-cognition one. BSA can leave literally zero free electives depending on concentration choice, making it the tightest schedule of the three.

BHA — Bachelor of Humanities and Arts (Cognitive Science + Music), the strong alternative. Total 378 units: 126 gen-ed, an 81–107-unit Dietrich concentration chosen from 31 options — Cognitive Science is explicitly listed — the CFA concentration, and 1–63 free electives (BHA curriculum). The BHA Cognitive Science concentration (Fall 2025) is a substantive 87-unit minimum (at least 9 courses), not a light minor: 15-112 programming; calculus (21-120 or the 21-111/112 sequence) and 21-127 Concepts of Mathematics; a statistics course (36-218/36-225/36-309/85-309); a three-course computational core — two of 15-122 Imperative Computation, 15-150 Functional Programming, 15-251 Theoretical CS, plus one cognitive-modeling course (85-412 Cognitive Modeling, 85-414, 85-419 Neural Computation, or 85-420); a four-course cognitive-psychology core (85-110/85-213, 85-300 research methods, and two advanced electives whose list includes machine learning, perception, logic, and music-cognition options); and one advisor-approved cognitive science elective. This is the variant prior research scored best for Eidee’s cognition-first direction: it keeps roughly two-thirds of the BCSA computing core and adds the psychology of mind, experimental methods, and modeling that the pure CS concentration omits — with the largest free-elective budget (up to 63 units) for law, ethics, and policy courses.

The non-BXA fallback. If violin should stay outside the degree, the safer CMU route is the Cognitive Science B.S. in Dietrich’s Psychology Department (core + pathway + a 27-unit designed concentration, drawing on psychology, linguistics, AI, philosophy, and neuroscience) combined with the SHIFT minor — formally the “Minor in Societal & Human Impacts of Future Technologies”, housed in the Philosophy Department: 8 courses / 72 units — two core courses (AI, Society, and Humanity and a SHIFT capstone) plus six area courses across Technology, Social & Behavioral Sciences, and Ethics, Policy & Design, with no double-counting limit. SHIFT is the most direct technology-law-and-policy credential CMU offers an undergraduate, and it pairs naturally with Cognitive Science. On this path, violin continues through lessons and ensembles outside the degree (see §6) with no admission audition.

4. How the pieces fit together

A BXA student’s week is genuinely split: studio lesson, ensemble rehearsals (the Philharmonic is a curricular requirement with seating auditions for string players each fall, per the ensembles syllabus), solfège/harmony classes, and then the CS/CogSci problem-set load on the other side of campus. The architecture is workable because the BXA gen-ed core is trimmed relative to single-college degrees and because the seminar/capstone sequence is shared — but total requirements of 378–380 units against a ~380-unit four-year budget mean the free-elective margin is the slack in the system. BHA preserves the most; BSA can preserve none.

Declaration timing: BXA students declare their specific academic concentration by spring mid-semester break of sophomore year (concentration declaration form; BHA/BSA pages). A student admitted to BHA can therefore arrive undecided between, say, Cognitive Science and Behavioral Economics. Changing the CFA concentration after arrival requires passing that CFA school’s full admission requirements again.

Gates beyond university admission 1. Violin prescreen video — competitive. Due December 1 via Acceptd; required of all violin applicants (violin requirements). 2. Live audition — competitive. Required for the Music Performance track; unaccompanied; by invitation after prescreen (BXA audition requirements). 3. BXA offer itself — competitive and separate. Indicating BXA interest does not guarantee a BXA offer; BXA notification follows the initial admission decision (BXA admission). 4. Academic concentration declaration — requirements-based (form by sophomore spring). 5. Ensemble seating auditions — requirements-based/competitive each year (placement, not membership). 6. Internal transfer into BXA (if not admitted as first-year) — competitive: requires a completed CMU semester, cumulative QPA 3.0, prerequisite coursework, a 500-word Essay of Interdisciplinary Intent, advisor sign-offs, and — critically — BXA music performance considers internal applicants only in spring for fall enrollment, with a new audition (internal transfer; next deadlines Oct 9, 2026 and Mar 5, 2027).

6. Beyond the classroom

Cognition and AI research. The Center for the Neural Basis of Cognition (CMU + University of Pittsburgh, since 1994) anchors Pittsburgh’s unusually deep two-university neuroscience ecosystem; BHA Cognitive Science students reach psychology and computational labs through 85-300 methods training and faculty contact, though no lab placement is ever guaranteed. BXA’s own office highlights student SURF research grants and alumni working “at the intersection of music and AI” (BXA programs).

Law, policy, ethics. CMU has no law school, but the Block Center for Technology and Society runs research on responsible AI, the future of work, and technology governance, with undergraduate entry points including a student policy fellowship and a research-assistant resume pool. Curricularly, the SHIFT minor and its Ethics, Policy & Design area are the most direct classroom complement; a BHA student can approximate SHIFT’s content through free electives even if the full minor proves hard to schedule alongside two concentrations.

Violin life. Inside BXA, violin life is the School of Music: weekly private studio (36 units across four semesters), the auditioned-seating CMU Philharmonic and companion ensembles, and performance electives. Outside a music degree (the fallback path), CMU fields an All University Orchestra that performs on the university events calendar; we could not verify current membership/lesson policies for non-majors on official pages, so confirm lesson access with the School of Music (music-admissions@andrew.cmu.edu) before relying on it.

7. Access, feasibility, and honest risks

The application is single-track. This is the structural risk that distinguishes CMU from every non-conservatory path in Eidee’s set: a BXA applicant applies through the School of Music and “will not be reviewed for a second college/program outside of your CFA choice” (BXA admission). There is no simultaneous Dietrich or SCS application. If the audition outcome is negative, the CMU application effectively ends for that cycle. And even a successful School of Music admit is not automatically a BXA admit — the BXA decision arrives separately.

The audition is a conservatory audition. The violin prescreen and live round (memorized Paganini/Kreutzer-class etude, Romantic concerto movement, solo Bach) are judged by a professional string faculty against applicants who intend performance careers. “Advanced” must mean advanced at that standard.

The schedule is real. Two concentrations plus a shared capstone consume essentially the whole 378–380-unit budget. In BSA the free-elective line can literally read zero; in BCSA it is 0–33. Only BHA reliably protects space for the law/policy/ethics layer that motivates Eidee’s ten-year plan — which is why prior research ranked BHA Cognitive Science, not the more obvious BCSA, as the best-fit variant.

Law/policy is not in any BXA core. No BXA concentration teaches technology law. That content must come from electives or the SHIFT minor — and whether a BXA student can feasibly add the 8-course SHIFT minor is a scheduling question to put to BXA advising, not an entitlement.

When a different path wins. If violin should remain serious but extracurricular, the Cognitive Science B.S. + SHIFT minor in Dietrich is the better CMU application: no audition gate, test-flexible review of a strong academic file, the most direct tech-policy credential on campus, and prior research scored it within a rounding error of the BHA route. If building computational tools (including for music) becomes the core identity, BCSA overtakes BHA. BSA fits only if Eidee’s center of gravity moves to laboratory science.

8. Applying — logistics for Fall 2027

CMU uses the Common Application exclusively ($75 fee; waivers via Common App economic-need indicators; essay + three short-answer writing supplement; counselor evaluation + one teacher recommendation) (requirements). School of Music applicants additionally submit through the Acceptd portal (app.getacceptd.com/cmumusic) (audition & portfolio review).
